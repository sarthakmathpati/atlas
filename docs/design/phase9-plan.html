Atlas redesign plan

Phase 8 is done, so this is now the plan for Phase 9. It gives Atlas a calmer, warmer look for long study sessions, adds a focus layer that helps anyone concentrate, and adds an ADHD mode you switch on from the top-right corner of every page. Every choice rests on the research below, and every open question is decided. The same plan is written into BUILD_SPEC.md (section 12.10, F31, F32 and Phase 9) for the next session.

Features removed: none Your data: untouched Sources read: about 55 studies, reviews and guides Phase 9: five sessions

Three maps, three themes

Day is a survey sheet, Dusk an old atlas, Night a sea chart. Same layout, three moods for daylight, late evening and dark rooms, and every text color is contrast-checked.

A focus layer for everyone

One line of intention per focus block, a line that shortens as time passes, a dimmed screen around your work, notifications held for the break, and real break views.

ADHD mode

One switch shows only the task in front of you, with visible time, small steps, instant rewards and planned breaks. It leaves out popular ideas that research has not supported.

Where we start

What feels off today

Your screens are tidy and every number is correct. What makes them feel like a textbook is the treatment, and five things cause it.

  • Every block is the same bordered boxPlan, streak, stats and notices all get a white card with a header row and ruled lines, so nothing leads.
  • One blue for everythingActions, links, the heatmap, charts and all 18 map regions use blues and teals, so subjects blur together.
  • No focal pointThe first thing to do has the same weight as the fifth. Your eye has to read the whole page to start.
  • Small, dense text and many hairlines13 to 14 px text, lots of dividers and small buttons read like a form.
  • A bright navy night themeSaturated navy with near-white text is high glare in a dark room, and bright text on dark blurs for people with astigmatism.

The map is the exception. It has texture, color and a sense of place, and the plan carries that feeling to the rest of the app.

Principles

Six rules for the new look

Calm in the middle, warm at the edges

Lessons, code and drills stay plain, because extras inside learning material lower learning. Warmth, drawings and color live around them: Today, empty states, milestones.

One thing leads each screen

A single focal card or number per screen. Everything else steps back in size and tone.

Color always means something

Subject colors identify subjects. Amber, green and coral stay reserved for learning, strong and fading. One accent color marks actions.

Soft, never stark

Off-white paper and near-black ink, with no pure white or pure black. Rounded shapes, and tone instead of lines to separate things.

Match the room

Day, Night and Dusk themes. They follow your system setting or switch on their own at sunset and at a time you choose.

Progress is a journey

Routes that fill in, rings that close, landmarks on the map. Progress is always shown, never guilt.

Chosen direction

Survey Chosen

Inspired by topographic survey maps. It stretches the map you already like across the whole app, so Atlas feels like one place. Each theme is a different kind of map: Day is a survey sheet (green-gray paper, brown contour lines, spot heights), Dusk is an old atlas (dim warm paper, gold contour lines, a dotted grid of latitude and longitude), and Night is a sea chart (deep charcoal water, cool depth lines, faint soundings). Lake blue marks actions in all three, and every subject has its own soft map color.

Headings and big numbersBricolage Grotesque, a warm grotesque with optical sizes. It adds character without hurting legibility.

TextureContour lines drawn from a seed, faint, only in page heads, empty states and breaks. Never behind text you study.

Considered

Studio, set aside

Studio was a softer modern look with lavender-gray, white cards and an iris accent. It was pleasant but generic: it could belong to any study app, it had no Dusk theme, and it didn't connect to the map. Survey keeps one idea from it. Data-heavy screens, such as the problem table and the workspace, stay plain and quiet, with no texture.

The creative layer

Ideas that make Atlas yours and help you focus

These go beyond colors. Each one either turns your own data into the picture on screen, or removes a known source of distraction. Each card rates the evidence behind it. The ideas with weaker evidence are cheap, optional, or both.

Living terrain

The contour lines in Today's page head aren't random. Each focus subject is a hill whose height is its readiness, marked with a spot height such as "▲ DSA 57". As you learn, the land rises, so the picture changes week by week and is always yours. Look at the page head in the mockup above, and switch themes to see the three maps: spot heights by day, a dotted grid of latitude and longitude at dusk, depth soundings at night.

Today's page head, the dashboard, the welcome screen.

Subject emblems

Each subject gets a small contour emblem drawn from its own seed, in its own color. It marks the subject in page heads, chips and on the map, so every region has a face you learn to recognize.

Page heads, subject chips, map regions, stamps.

Summit profile

Readiness over time drawn like a hiking elevation profile. Your interview is the summit flag, and your projection is a dashed trail up to it.

Dashboard, next to the readiness ring. Example data.

Focus sessions

Starting the focus timer asks for one line, "In this block I will…", filled in from the plan item. While the block runs, a thin line along the top edge shortens as time passes, everything except your work dims, and notifications wait for the break. The line puts time in the corner of your eye without a number to watch.

Everywhere, whenever the focus timer runs. Dimming and held notifications can be turned off.

Binary search on the answer

When the answer is a number and you can check any guess (is it too big, or big enough?), search the answer space instead of the input. The check must be monotonic: once a guess works, every larger guess works too.

For an integer square root, the check is "mid × mid ≤ x", and the answer is the largest mid that passes.

Park it

One key (p) captures a stray thought with a when: at the break, tonight or tomorrow. Parked items come back at that time, and tonight's can become items on tomorrow's plan. Making a plan for an unfinished task stops it from pulling at your attention.

A shortcut on every page, the focus timer, the break view, and the Now card in ADHD mode.

Pencil and ink

Concepts you haven't started are written in pencil: lighter, with a dashed mark. Learned ones are inked. The map's own "inked in" idea carries into every list.

Break views

A break fills the screen with a calm landscape drawn in the theme's lines, one eye-rest or movement idea, and an optional minute of slow breathing: a ring that grows for five seconds and shrinks for five, about six breaths a minute. The break's remaining time is the tide line at the bottom.

Focus timer breaks, the interview day, the ADHD mode break check-in.

Look at something far away for 20 seconds.

Memory walk

Flashcards for a topic come in the order of their places on the map, with a small strip showing where you are on the route, so you revisit ideas as places. The method of loci works well; applying it to review order is our own extension, so this is the least proven idea here and costs little.

Flashcards and reviews for a topic or subject.

Weekly stamps

Each week with five or more active days adds a stamp to your logbook, marked with the emblem of the subject you studied most. Stamps never expire and are never taken away.

Dashboard and the weekly review.

Interview day

On the day before and the day of an interview, Today becomes calm: the 1-day revision sheet, your mistake checklist, a minute of slow breathing, and a place to park worries with a plan. There's nothing new to learn. Writing about test worries helped in one famous study, but two large replications found no effect, so the app makes no promise about it.

Today, when an interview date is set.

Themes by the clock

With "By time of day", Atlas moves to Dusk in the evening and Night later. Once you set a bedtime, a quiet note appears half an hour before it and offers to park what's left and plan tomorrow.

Settings, the top bar's theme menu.

Structure

Screen by screen

Every feature, button and number stays. What changes is how each screen is composed.

App frame

The sidebar sits on tinted paper with no dividing line, the current page is a filled pill, and your focus subjects appear as colored links. The top bar holds search, the focus timer, the streak, the ADHD mode switch and Ask Claude.

Today

A page head with the greeting, your living terrain, the countdown drawn as a map scale bar, and a ring for today's minutes. One large "Up next" card with a big Start. The rest of the plan becomes "Today's route": stops on a dotted line that fill in as you finish them. The streak becomes a 7-day strip, and "Ready to learn" becomes cards in subject colors.

Dashboard

Sections of different sizes instead of equal boxes. The readiness ring is split into subject colors, with the summit profile beside it and weekly stamps below. Subject bars use subject colors and icons, as shown above. Pattern tiles become a softer field of tiles, and charts use the same palette. Every number still opens its explanation.

Map

Layout, zoom levels and behavior stay the same. Regions take the new subject colors, and the paper, grid and contours follow the theme. It's the part you like, now with 18 distinct regions.

Problems

It stays a table because it is data. Rows get more height, filters become pills, difficulty and status become chips, and each row starts with its subject color.

Workspace

Quiet on purpose. The editor theme matches Day, Night or Dusk, the timer is larger, and hint, review and dry-run panels become soft trays under the editor.

Concept reading

A reading column of 60 to 70 characters, 17 to 18 px text with 1.6 line height, headings in the display face, and interview points in their own card. A short "Check yourself" block closes each level. There's no decoration inside the lesson.

Review, flashcards, drill

Cards that feel like cards: bigger, centered, turning over to show the answer, with keyboard hints. A calm finish screen shows what changed.

Weekly and revision

The weekly review reads like a logbook page. Revision sheets keep their print styles, and their screen view takes the reading layout.

Practice screens

The Phase 8 screens (mock interviews, designs, stories, puzzles, mental math) take the same treatment. Timed rounds show the horizon line, and the sketch canvas uses the theme's paper and ink.

Settings

Theme: System, Day, Dusk, Night or By time of day, with the Dusk and Night start times and an optional bedtime. Focus sessions: length, dimming, held notifications, break views and breathing. ADHD mode and each of its parts.

Moments

Small line drawings in the map style (compass, trail, flag) for empty states. The ink moment when a concept turns strong stays the signature animation. Finishing the day draws the route to its flag. All motion follows the reduced-motion setting.

ADHD mode

What research says helps with ADHD

ADHD mainly affects self-regulation: judging time, holding steps in mind, starting, and staying with tasks whose reward is far away. Clinicians recommend supports that sit exactly where the work happens. Most studies are on children and teens, and adult studies are fewer, so each finding below says who was studied.

ADHD mode

What the switch does

The switch sits in the top bar's right corner on every page, Today and Dashboard included. The first time you turn it on, a short card lists what changed. Each part can be turned off in Settings, and the choice syncs with the rest of your data. Try it in the mockup below: tick steps, park a thought, and watch the ink fill. Some supports now belong to everyone through the focus layer (intentions, parking thoughts, planned breaks). ADHD mode adds the calm screen, the Now card, visible time everywhere, instant rewards, reading support, focus sound and Study with Claude.

Today with ADHD mode on (Survey direction)

What I'd leave out, and why

Bionic readingBolding the first half of each word gave no gain in speed or comprehension in controlled tests.
Colored reading overlaysEvidence is weak even for dyslexia, where they are most often sold.
Red countdowns, lost streaks, penaltiesThey add stress and shame, which many people with ADHD already carry. Rewards are only ever added.
Confetti and constant motionMoving extras pull attention away harder in ADHD. Feedback animations stay short and small.
Focus sounds on by defaultNoise helped people with ADHD a little and hurt people without it, so it waits until you choose it.

A note on scope. ADHD mode supports study habits. It doesn't treat ADHD and doesn't replace medical care. Anyone can use it, and people who find focus hard often like it.

Decided

Decisions made

  1. Survey, with three map themes.It's calmer for long hours, it has a Dusk theme, and it extends the map you like. Data-heavy screens stay plain, the one idea kept from Studio.
  2. Dusk and themes by the clock, yes.Theme options: System (Day or Night), Day, Dusk, Night, and By time of day (Dusk from 19:00, Night from 22:30 by default). The bedtime note stays off until you set a bedtime. Dusk promises comfort, not better sleep.
  3. No mascot.Friendly faces helped younger learners most. Subject emblems and map-style line drawings give warmth and double as subject identity.
  4. Every ADHD mode part, with two starting off.Focus sound and Study with Claude start off even in ADHD mode: they are the least proven, and noise hurt people without ADHD.
  5. A focus layer for everyone.Intentions, the horizon line, dimming, held notifications, Park it and break views help anyone who studies for hours, so they don't wait behind the ADHD switch.
  6. The spec's rules still hold.Sentence case everywhere (the mockup's labels were changed to match), status shapes, plain copy, reduced motion, and one decorative animation, the ink moment. The route's ink stroke, the horizon line and the breathing ring are feedback and tools, not decoration.
  7. Order inside Phase 9.Foundations, screens, focus layer and ADHD mode come before the final polish, so the audit and the last screenshots cover the finished look.
